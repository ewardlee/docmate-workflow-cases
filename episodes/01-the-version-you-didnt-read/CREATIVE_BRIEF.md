# Episode 01 — The Version You Didn't Read

## Status
ACTIVE PRODUCTION — all later episodes paused.

## Creative rule
This is a short drama first, a product film second.

The viewer should understand and care about the human problem before DocMate appears.

## Premise
A couple is preparing to move into a new apartment. Their landlord sends a revised lease with a casual message:

> "Everything's basically the same. Just the usual renewal."

They are excited about the move and are about to sign.

One quiet clause changed.

The change affects something emotionally important to them.

## Core dramatic choice
The lease change is not merely a fee or date. It changes whether their dog can remain with them.

Original:
> One small domestic pet is permitted with prior written approval.

Revised:
> Pets are not permitted without a separate written exception from the landlord.

The couple has had the dog for years.

This makes the document consequence human:
**one line could force a choice between the apartment and a member of the family.**

## Theme
Trust people. Check documents.

## Tone
Naturalistic, restrained, intimate.

No melodrama.
No evil landlord.
No "AI magic".
No corporate advertising energy.

The landlord may simply have reused a standard lease template. The point is not deception; the point is that important changes can hide inside routine revisions.

## Characters

### Maya
Early 30s.
Practical, warm, slightly impatient because the move has already consumed weeks of her life.

### Daniel
Early 30s.
Calm, detail-oriented, initially thinks the paperwork is routine.

### Milo
Their older dog.
Never treated as a cute prop. His presence establishes the stakes before the document reveal.

## Location
Small apartment at night during packing.

Half-packed boxes.
Tape gun on the floor.
A floor plan or measuring tape.
Quiet domestic realism.

## Story structure

### Act 1 — The future they are already imagining
Maya measures a wall for the sofa.

Daniel is opening boxes / checking his phone.

Milo is asleep nearby.

They talk about where Milo's bed will go in the new place.

This establishes the emotional stake without explaining it.

### Act 2 — The routine document
Phone buzzes.

Landlord:
> "Sent the final renewal. Everything's basically the same. Sign when you get a chance."

Daniel opens the PDF.

He scrolls quickly.
Stops at the signature page.

Maya, off-screen:
> "Can we just get this done tonight?"

He almost signs.

### Act 3 — The tiny hesitation
Daniel notices the filename is slightly different from the previous lease.

No dramatic music cue.

He opens DocMate.

This is the first time the product appears.

### Act 4 — The reveal
Authentic DocMate Compare.

A couple of harmless changes first:
- rent amount
- lease dates

Then:

Pet clause changed.

The room goes quiet.

Cut to Milo sleeping beside a half-packed box.

No explanatory voiceover.

### Act 5 — The decision
Daniel opens the source clause.

Maya reads it over his shoulder.

She says only:
> "He never mentioned that."

Daniel adds a Review note:
> "Please confirm pet exception before signing."

They do not sign.

They send a clarification request instead.

### Act 6 — Aftermath
Milo wakes and walks between the boxes.

No triumphant ending.

Black screen:

> It was one line.

Then:

> Before you sign, know what changed.

DocMate

## Why this works
The app does not "save the day" in a cartoonish way.

It changes a decision at the right moment.

The viewer understands the value without being told:
- why versions matter
- why Compare matters
- why Review matters
- why preserving the original matters
- why "Sign the right version" matters
